Profession conventions

What we change for writing and media

Portfolio URL in the header

Your site or clip portfolio link goes next to your email in the header. An editor opens it before reading anything else — it needs to be one click away, not buried in a footer.

Clips organised by beat and outlet

Not a chronological list of jobs. Your best 4–6 clips, named and linked, organised by the beat you want to be hired for. The piece that triggered a parliamentary inquiry goes at the top.

Bylines and outlet prestige

The Guardian, Financial Times, The Atlantic — named prominently. For journalists, where you have published is as important as what you have published.

Investigations and impact

Regulatory responses, corrections, retractions you triggered in others, reader numbers — impact that goes beyond the word count. We frame your work by what it changed, not just what it covered.

Awards and recognition

Paul Foot, Pulitzer, BSME, British Journalism Awards — with year and category. Shortlists count. We list them the way awards bodies expect to see them cited.

Languages and regions

For international journalists — languages, regions covered, sources cultivated across borders. A West Africa correspondent with six-country source networks is a different candidate from a London desk reporter.

Preparation

What we will ask you for

Have these ready and the first draft moves faster.

  • 01Current CV or LinkedIn in any state
  • 02Portfolio URL or clip links — 4–6 best pieces with outlet names
  • 03Any investigations that triggered real-world responses
  • 04Awards and shortlists — name, year, category
  • 05Roles you are targeting — publication names or job titles
