The best resume format for a fresher in India is a one-page, single-column document that opens with your contact details, then education, then projects and internships, then skills. Everything on it should show what you can already do; anything that only describes who you are (photo, date of birth, father's name, a signed declaration) can go.

The right order of sections

Recruiters reading a fresher's resume ask two questions: what have you studied, and what have you built or done? Answer in that order.

  1. Name and contact details. Full name, phone number, a professional email address, city, and a LinkedIn link. Add a GitHub, Behance or portfolio link if your field uses one.
  2. Summary (optional, two or three lines). What you are studying, what you are good at, and the kind of role you want. Skip it rather than write a generic one.
  3. Education. For a fresher this is your strongest credential, so it goes near the top.
  4. Projects and internships. Your substitute for work experience, written the same way experience would be.
  5. Skills. Technical skills, tools and languages, grouped.
  6. Certifications and training. Only the ones relevant to the job.
  7. Positions of responsibility and activities. Clubs, fests, sports, NSS, student bodies.
  8. Achievements (optional). Ranks, scholarships, hackathon or competition results.

If you have a substantial internship (three months or more in a relevant role), you can place it above education. Once you have a year or two of full-time work, experience moves to the top for good.

How long should it be?

One page. A final-year student or recent graduate rarely has enough relevant material to fill two pages without padding, and padding is what recruiters skim past. If you are over a page, cut the weakest project and shorten bullet points to one or two lines. Tighten margins only after cutting words; a crammed page is harder to read than a trimmed one.

What goes in place of work experience

You do have experience. It just did not come with a salary. Treat each of the following like a job entry: a title, the organisation, dates, and two or three bullet points describing what you did and what came of it.

  • Internships. Name the company, your role and the dates. Say what you worked on, not what the team did. "Cleaned and merged three years of sales data in Excel for the regional review" beats "Assisted the sales team".
  • Academic and personal projects. Final-year projects, mini-projects, case studies, research papers, apps you built for yourself. State the problem, the tools you used and the result. Link to the code or a write-up if there is one.
  • Training and live projects. Industrial training, articleship for CA students, clinical postings for nursing and pharmacy students, summer schools.
  • College roles. Placement coordinator, club secretary, fest organiser, class representative. These show responsibility, organisation and dealing with people, which employers do look for in freshers.
  • Freelance and part-time work. Tutoring, content writing, a small design job. If it was real work for a real person, it counts.

Use numbers where you honestly have them: team size, users, marks, budget, time saved. Do not invent them.

How to write the education section

For each qualification, give the degree and specialisation, the college and university, the years, and your score. Write the score the way your institution reports it: CGPA out of 10 or a percentage, and say which.

An entry might read: B.Tech, Computer Science and Engineering, XYZ Institute of Technology, Pune (Savitribai Phule Pune University), 2023 – 2027 (expected), CGPA 8.2 / 10 up to semester 6.

Class 10 and 12: include them as a fresher. Many campus recruiters and large employers in India screen on 10th and 12th percentages, and their application forms ask for them anyway. One line each is enough: board, school, year, percentage. Drop them once you have a postgraduate degree or a few years of work.

If your CGPA is low, you may leave it off the resume, but expect the form or the interviewer to ask. Do not round it up.

Skills and certifications

Group skills so they are easy to scan: for example, Languages: Python, Java, SQL and Tools: Git, Power BI, Tally, AutoCAD. List only what you could use in an interview tomorrow. "Basic" knowledge of twelve languages reads worse than working knowledge of three.

Leave out vague soft skills such as "hard-working" and "team player". Show them through your projects and college roles instead.

For certifications, give the name, the issuing body and the year. Prefer examined ones, such as NPTEL courses, NISM modules for finance roles, or vendor certifications for cloud and networking. Two relevant, examined certificates count for more than ten completion badges.

What to leave out, and why

Many Indian templates still carry sections from the older "biodata" format. Most employers no longer need them.

  • Photo. Not needed for most private-sector jobs in India, and it invites judgements unrelated to the work. Add one only if the employer asks, or for roles such as acting and modelling where appearance is part of the job.
  • Date of birth, gender, marital status, religion, nationality. Not relevant to whether you can do the job. If an employer needs them for eligibility, their application form will ask.
  • Father's name and permanent address. Your city is enough. A full home address adds nothing and shares more than you need to.
  • "Declaration" and signature lines. "I hereby declare that the above information is true" is implied by sending the resume. Recruiters do not read it, and it costs you three lines.
  • Objective clichés. "Seeking a challenging position in a reputed organisation where I can utilise my skills" says nothing about you. Replace it with a specific two-line summary, or delete it.
  • Hobbies with no story. "Reading, music, travelling" fills space. Keep an interest only if it shows something, such as a state-level chess rating.

Government and PSU applications are the exception: they often ask for personal details in a prescribed format. Follow their form exactly.

File format and naming

Send a PDF unless the employer asks for Word. It keeps your layout identical on every screen. Export it from Word or Google Docs rather than scanning a printout; if you can select and copy the text, it is fine.

Name the file with your name and the document type, for example Sneha-Patil-Resume.pdf. Never send resume_final_v3.pdf or CV.pdf; the recruiter has hundreds of those. Our guide to the ATS-friendly resume covers PDF versus DOCX in more detail.

A worked example outline

Here is how a final-year B.Com student applying for audit and finance trainee roles might lay out one page:

  1. Header: Sneha Patil, Pune, phone, email, LinkedIn.
  2. Summary: Final-year B.Com student specialising in accounting, with a two-month audit internship and working knowledge of Tally and advanced Excel, looking for an audit or finance trainee role.
  3. Education: B.Com, college and university, 2024 – 2027 (expected), percentage to date. Then class 12 and class 10, one line each.
  4. Internship: Audit intern at a CA firm, two months. Three bullets: vouching and ledger scrutiny for named types of client, the reconciliation she prepared, the software used.
  5. Projects: Final-year project on working-capital ratios of listed FMCG companies, with the data source and method.
  6. Skills: Tally, GST return basics, Excel (pivot tables, lookups), English, Hindi and Marathi.
  7. Certifications: One relevant, examined course (for example an NPTEL course in accounting or taxation), with the year.
  8. Positions of responsibility: Treasurer of the college commerce society, with the budget handled.

The same skeleton works for engineering, science and arts graduates. See full examples on our sample CVs page, and how expectations differ between fields on the professions page.

Common mistakes

  • Using a two-column or graphic-heavy template that application systems read out of order. Our templates are all single-column for this reason.
  • Listing duties ("responsible for testing") instead of what you did and what happened.
  • One generic resume for every application. Adjust the summary, skills order and top project to match each job description.
  • Spelling mistakes, especially in the company's name or your own college's, and an unprofessional email address.
  • Rating yourself with stars or bars ("Python: 4/5"). They mean nothing to a reader and systems cannot read them.

Questions people ask

Should a fresher's resume be one page or two?

One page. Freshers rarely have enough relevant material for two. Cut weaker projects and shorten bullet points rather than shrinking the font.

Should I put my photo on my resume in India?

For most private-sector jobs, no. It says nothing about your ability and uses space. Add one only if the employer asks or appearance is part of the role.

Do I need to include class 10 and 12 marks?

Yes, as a fresher. Many Indian employers screen on them and ask for them in application forms. Give one line each with board, year and percentage, and drop them once you have a postgraduate degree or some years of work.

Is a declaration required at the end of a resume?

No. Sending the resume already implies the information is true. The declaration and signature lines are a leftover from the old biodata format and can be removed, unless a government form specifically asks for them.

What should I write if I have no internship?

Use academic and personal projects, industrial training, freelance or part-time work, and college roles. Write each like a job: what you did, the tools you used and the result.
