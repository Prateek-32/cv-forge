Profession conventions

What we change for artists

Your work, large and first

A portfolio site that opens on your best piece, then a gallery grouped into series — tap any work and it fills the screen. Room views show scale; a signature piece stands in for a headshot.

Title, medium, size, year

Every work is labelled the way galleries label it, with Sold, Available or Commission beside it. Buyers stop guessing, and the enquiries you get are the right ones.

An artist statement, not a bio

Three or four lines on what you make and why — the subject, the material, the way you work. No "passionate artist since childhood"; the work says that.

Shows, commissions and collections

Solo and group shows with venue and year, fairs and markets, residencies, notable commissions and where your work hangs — each as its own list, newest first.

Numbers buyers trust

Pieces sold, commissions completed, editions, repeat customers, workshop students, ratings. Follower counts only where they bring in orders — and we say how many.

A clear way to buy or book you

How a commission works, what workshops you teach, where you ship and how to reach you. Your shop, Instagram and website sit in the header of your CV, next to your email.

Preparation

What we will ask you for

Have these ready and your portfolio moves faster.

  • 0115–30 photos of your best work — daylight, straight on, no filters; photographers send full-resolution files
  • 02Title, medium, size, year and status (sold, available, commission) for each piece
  • 03Exhibitions, fairs, markets and residencies, with years
  • 04Commissions, workshops, sales and notable buyers or clients
  • 05Your Instagram, shop or website links, and your current CV in any state
