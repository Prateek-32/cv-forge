        How it works

        What the checker looks at

        The same things applicant tracking systems and recruiters trip over.

        Can it be read?

        Scanned images, two-column layouts, tables, text boxes, contact details hidden in the page header and icon fonts that turn into boxes.

        Is it all there?

        Email, phone with country code and LinkedIn; standard Experience, Education and Skills sections; a summary instead of an objective.

        Does it persuade?

        Results with numbers, action verbs instead of "responsible for", no clichés or personal details, one date format, a sensible length — and, with a job description, the keywords it asks for.

        Questions

        About the ATS checker

        What is an ATS resume check?

        An applicant tracking system (ATS) is the software many employers use to collect and search applications. An ATS check tests whether that software can read your resume correctly — layout, sections, contact details and keywords — so you are not filtered out for reasons that have nothing to do with your experience.

        Is this ATS checker really free?

        Yes. The score, the category breakdown and your top fixes are free, with no sign-up. The full report — every fix plus notes from a person — is ₹49.

        Is my resume uploaded anywhere?

        No. The free check runs entirely in your browser. Your resume is only sent to Fieldcraft if you choose to get the full report, and then only to prepare it.

        What score should my resume get?

        Aim for 80 or above. Below 60 usually means something structural — a two-column layout, a scanned file or missing sections — that is worth fixing before you apply anywhere.

        Which file should I upload, PDF or Word?

        Either. Check the file you actually send to employers. A text-based PDF or a .docx both work; an old .doc or a photo of your resume does not.
