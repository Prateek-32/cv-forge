Our story

Three generations, one kettle of chai

Parvaneh means “butterfly” in Persian. Grandpa said the café was named for the way customers flit in for a chai and somehow stay all morning.

  1. 1958

    Four tables and a brass kettle

    Khodadad, twenty-three and newly arrived from Yazd, rents a corner room off M.G. Road. Chai, bun maska and a house rule or two.

  2. 1984

    The oven goes in

    His son Jehangir builds a wood-fired oven out back. The butter biscuits sell out by eleven. They still do.

  3. 2019

    Shirin takes the counter

    His granddaughter swaps a bank job in Mumbai for the cash drawer, keeps every recipe, and adds exactly one rule to the board.

  4. Today

    Same chai, new tricks

    Same marble tables, same clock (still five minutes fast), plus bakery boxes on WhatsApp and a QR menu on every table.

House rules

Every Irani café has a board

Grandpa chalked ours in 1958, and it has been rewritten, word for word, every Monday since. Most of it still holds. Some of it we are still negotiating.

Rule no. 8 is Shirin’s. Grandpa would have approved.

House Rules

by order of the management
  1. No combing hair.
  2. No discussing betting.Yes, even on race day.
  3. No credit. Ask tomorrow.
  4. Pay at counter.
  5. No sitting long after one chai.Unless it is raining. Then stay.
  6. No bargaining. The price is the price.
  7. No feet on chairs.They are older than you.
  8. No laptops at lunch hour (12 – 3).

— The Management, 1958 (& 2019)
