Profession conventions

What we change for trades

Licences and tickets first

Red Seal, journeyman certificate, OSHA 30, WHMIS, First Aid — in a scannable strip above the summary. A site supervisor checks these before reading anything else.

Sector stated explicitly

Residential, light commercial, heavy industrial — stated in every job entry. Project managers use this to assess fit. "Electrical work" tells them nothing; "600V industrial distribution" does.

Project value and scope

CAD $28M data centre, hospital expansion, transit infrastructure — sized and named. Trades professionals who can quote project value and scope signal commercial awareness beyond the tools.

Safety record

Zero lost-time injuries across 11 years and 140,000 crew hours — this is a credibility signal that senior tradespeople earn and that most CVs fail to state. We make it prominent.

Union and prevailing wage

IBEW, UA, UBC membership and local number — stated where applicable. Whether you have worked on union or Davis-Bacon/prevailing wage sites is often a hiring prerequisite, not a preference.

Apprentices supervised

Number of apprentices mentored and their outcomes. For supervisor and foreperson roles, training the next generation is a core responsibility — we surface it explicitly.

Preparation

What we will ask you for

Have these ready and the first draft moves faster.

  • 01Current resume in any state
  • 02All licences and tickets with certificate numbers and expiry dates
  • 03Largest projects — value, sector, your role, and any notable outcomes
  • 04Safety record — LTI history and total crew hours if available
  • 05Union membership details and any prevailing wage experience
